This summary outlines the key discussions and decisions from a committee
meeting, which focused on two main agenda items: the stressed Loss Given Default
(LGD) approach for ICAP 2027 and a self-assessment of the Model Oversight
Committee (MOCK) itself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I. Stressed LGD Approach for ICAP 2027

The first agenda item involved a review and validation of a new stressed LGD
approach for the upcoming ICAP (Internal Capital Adequacy Process) exercise in
2027.

 * Background & Rationale:
   
   * Previously, the bank’s SEL models used conservative LGD assumptions (60%
     for secured corporate portfolios, 100% for unsecured).
   
   * Model governance mandated a review of these LGD assumptions, leading to an
     expert judgment approach for estimating stressed LGDs.
   
   * The goal is to move from assumptions to a data-backed approach.

 * UKRM Assessment & Findings:
   
   * The UK Risk Management (UKRM) team validated the approach, noting it as a
     "data quality model" (DQM) rather than a full model.
   
   * Two limitations were identified: requiring enhanced model documentation and
     improved ongoing monitoring.
   
   * Two observations were raised, but the materiality of the issues was deemed
     medium, and the quality score was good, resulting in a low overall risk
     rating.

 * Methodology:
   
   * Basic Scenario: Utilises a Long Run Average LGD (LRA LGD) calculated from
     historical data spanning 2009 to 2025, averaging LGDs for both resolved and
     unresolved cases, with a 12-month window for case classification.
   
   * Stressed Scenario: A scalar is calculated based on historical deviations
     (differences between specific historical LGD points and the LRA LGD). This
     scalar is then applied to the LRA LGD to derive the stressed LGD.

 * Key Discussions & Concerns:
   
   * The new LGDs, while data-driven, could result in lower credit losses
     compared to previous conservative assumptions, prompting a need for
     sensitivity analysis.
   
   * There is still only one stressed LGD applied, similar to past practice.
   
   * The impact of elevated defaults (e.g., due to operational strain) that
     cured without significant loss was discussed; while increasing default
     population, these cases would tend to lower the long-run average LGD.
   
   * The data used (FREX tables, 'impa') aligns with IFRS 9 data sources.
   
   * Sensitivity Analysis: A comparison was made with a "point-in-time" LGD
     approach that incorporated macro variables (GDP, HPI). The static LGD
     approach was chosen for ICAP 2027 because the observed variability in
     losses between the two approaches was not material.

 * Decision & Future Monitoring:
   
   * The approach was recommended for approval.
   
   * Ongoing monitoring requirements: The LRA LGD must be calibrated annually,
     and the approach reviewed before each ICAP submission, incorporating new
     default and recovery data.
   
   * Observations for improvement:
     
     * Unresolved cases were concentrated in higher recovery time bands, with
       LGD floor gaps not applied, and some cases showing unusually long
       recovery times (e.g., over 200 months post-default) with high LGDs, which
       needs further investigation.
     
     * An initial calculation error where the SPL segment was incorrectly
       included in LRA LGD calculation was noted and fixed, with no material
       impact.

II. Committee Self-Assessment (MOCK)

The second part of the meeting involved a self-assessment of the Model Oversight
Committee, a new process where assessment is done collectively rather than
individually.

 * Overall Process Change: Self-assessment is now conducted within the
   committee, promoting open and honest discussion.

 * Key Areas of Review & Feedback:
   
   * Purpose of the Committee:
     
     * Feedback: The committee is clear on its purpose (embedding MRM, ensuring
       proper model governance). However, there's a need for improved feedback
       loops to ALCO, particularly regarding challenges to assumptions and the
       reasonableness of model outputs in liquidity and capital models.
     
     * Action: The committee will explore how to better present and challenge
       model inputs and outputs, acknowledging time constraints and the role of
       other committees (ALCO, Credit Oversight Committee).
   
   * Membership & Capabilities:
     
     * Feedback: The current membership is considered appropriate, with diverse
       skills and experience. Future focus areas (AML, wealth models) will
       necessitate continued attention to model owners' understanding of
       business use and technicalities.
     
     * Action: More training for committee members, particularly on financial
       crime and wealth models, was requested. An induction for new joiners and
       an offline MRM training session will be considered.
   
   * Risk Intelligence & Escalation:
     
     * Feedback: The committee effectively challenges models and encourages
       differing views, applying risk-based tiering. Concern was raised about
       the increasing volume of IRB models potentially overwhelming the Board
       Risk Committee (BRCC) with technical details. The aim is to focus BRCC
       discussions on model use and confidence.
     
     * Action: Work with the governance team to ensure appropriate content is
       escalated to BRCC, potentially considering a subgroup for initial IRB
       model discussions.
   
   * Meetings & Materials:
     
     * Feedback: Meetings are held regularly (monthly, with some exceptions),
       and attendance is generally good. A recurring issue is late submission
       and last-minute changes to papers, primarily from the IRB world.
     
     * Action: Encourage timely submissions from all members. A suggestion to
       focus committee discussions primarily on high and medium-materiality
       findings, rather than low-materiality ones, was accepted.
   
   * Information Resources & Recordings:
     
     * Feedback: Meeting minutes are accurate, reflect discussions, challenges,
       and decisions, and are circulated promptly. Actions are clearly
       documented and tracked.
   
   * Reporting & Escalation Structure:
     
     * Discussion: There was some confusion regarding MOCK's official
       hierarchical relationship with the MRCC (Management Risk Committee)
       versus its practical reporting to BRCC. While terms of reference might
       suggest MOCK as a subcommittee of MRCC, in practice, MOCK reports
       directly to BRCC, especially for IRB matters. Frameworks and aggregate
       risk insights (tolerances, portfolio summaries) are escalated to MRCC.
     
     * Action: Clarify the exact reporting structure with the governance team.
   
   * Chair Effectiveness:
     
     * Feedback: The committee is chaired effectively, with appropriate time
       dedicated to key matters, and opportunities for all to contribute.
       Chairing duties are often delegated.
   
   * Future Focus & Emerging Risks:
     
     * Feedback: The committee needs to be more forward-looking. Emerging risks
       like Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the impact of external events
       (e.g., Covid forbearance) on models will require significant attention.
     
     * Action: Integrate horizon scanning from UKRM to identify potential model
       impacts. Proactively bring papers on approaches for anticipated
       model-breaking events.
   
   * Key Areas for Next 12 Months:
     
     * The greatest attention will be on AI and wealth integration (including
       setting appropriate tolerances and flagging risks).
     
     * Continued focus on rolling out AML and wealth models.
   
   * Focus on Assumptions & Data:
     
     * Feedback: Board feedback indicated a need for greater focus on the
       reasonableness and evidence of assumptions and data robustness within
       models.
     
     * Action: Rethink how assumptions and data are presented and challenged
       within the MOCK, ensuring discussions go beyond technical details to
       evaluate business context and output reasonableness.

 * General Committee Sentiment & Outlook:
   
   * The overall sentiment was positive regarding the committee's function, but
     with clear areas identified for improvement, particularly in communication,
     process efficiency, and proactive engagement with emerging risks.
   
   * The upcoming November MOCK meeting is anticipated to be "model-heavy,"
     featuring discussions on the "Rover" model and fraud monitoring.
   
   * Ongoing Education: A commitment was made to introduce educational pieces to
     the MOCK going forward, especially concerning retail IRB model development
     and AI/ML concepts, to familiarise members with new areas before formal
     model validations.
